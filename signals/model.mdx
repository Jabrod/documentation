title: "The model"
description: "How Signals trains, validates and explains its predictions."
---

You get one model, trained only on your data.

## Features

For each customer, as of a given date, Signals computes:

- Tenure, plan and monthly revenue.
- Days since the last usage event; events in the last 7 and 30 days; active days in the last 30.
- **Usage trend**: the last 3 weeks against the 3 weeks before, as a percentage.
- Distinct features used in 30 days, and days since the most telling event was last used.
- Failed payments in 60 and 90 days; days since the last successful payment.
- Support tickets in 30 days, and their trend.

A feature only uses data dated on or before that date. The model never sees the future.

## Training

1. **Snapshots.** Every 14 days through your history, Signals takes each active customer, computes their features as of that day, and records whether they churned within the prediction window after it.
2. **Time split.** The most recent fifth of snapshot dates is held back for testing. The model trains only on earlier dates.
3. **Model.** A small, regularised gradient-boosted model (LightGBM), with its probabilities calibrated so that "62%" means roughly 62 in 100.
4. **Final fit.** After testing, the model is retrained on all snapshots and scores every active customer.

## Quality numbers

Each report shows how the model did on the held-back months:

| Number | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| ROC AUC | How well it ranks leavers above stayers. 0.5 is a coin flip; 1.0 is perfect. |
| PR AUC | The same, weighted toward the customers you would act on. |
| Precision in the top 10% | Of the customers it flagged, how many really left. |
| Lift | How much better than picking customers at random. |
| Base rate | How many customers churn in a normal window. |
| Counts | Customers, snapshots and churn events behind the numbers. |

Two warnings appear when they apply:

- **Not enough history**: under 3 months of data or about 50 churn events.
- **Possible leakage**: an AUC above 0.97 usually means a column is only filled in after a customer leaves.

## Reasons

Every score comes with the two or three things pushing that customer's risk up most, measured with SHAP values and written from templates with the customer's own numbers:

- "Usage down 62% in 3 weeks"
- "Two failed payments in 60 days"
- "No activity in 18 days"
- "Support tickets up, logins down"
- "Hasn't used Reports in 30 days"
- "Joined 41 days ago"

No AI model writes the reasons, and no customer data is sent to one.
